# Private creator outreach engine

## Need, value and limits

The maintainer requested a daily goal of at least 150 relevant influencer first contacts, with growth treated as a core operating priority. The existing November 10 private ledger has three provider-confirmed first sends. Delivery, replies and useful external participation have not been verified. Earlier entry-page experiments do not provide acquisition denominators or evidence of causal lift.

The real task is to help relevant creators find one research question worth answering without duplicate invitations, invented familiarity or unwanted follow-ups. This change adds a small local ledger and CLI, not a sending service, scraper, public CRM or new signup requirement. The hypothesis is that evidence-led invitations produce useful corrections and reuse. Tests establish bookkeeping, not growth or benefit.

P1/P2: each invitation needs a cited relevant work and a specific small ask. P5: refusal permanently suppresses the creator and linked endpoints; unknown sends stop rather than retry. P6: count only distinct creators with provider evidence, separating message sends from accepted contact forms; distinguish sending, delivery, reply and actual participation. P3/P4: no governance, credit, credential or publication rights change. The maintainer remains the operator under v1 controls. Activity targets never override these boundaries.

## Storage and operation

`python3 -m arc.growth --db /private/path/growth.sqlite ...` uses Python's standard library and SQLite. Use a private directory **outside the checkout and the public site**. Files are created owner-only; keep the containing directory private. `.gitignore` is a defense in depth, not permission to store real contacts in Git. Public reports contain aggregate counts only. Do not publish contact lists, message bodies, source-to-person mappings or private outcome evidence.

The runner owns authorized discovery, source verification and sending through connected tools. This CLI has **no network, email, scraping, scheduling or credential functionality**. A reservation is not authorization and is not a sent message. Public contact details alone are not universal permission for bulk marketing. Check the channel's current rules, the contact's stated purpose, applicable rules, account permissions and sender capacity. Never evade limits with more accounts or channels. No paid placement, sponsorship, contract or other commitment is included.

Before each daily session, restore the most recent private checkpoint and reconcile provider sent history, replies, bounces, refusals and unresolved reservations. The private checkpoint must be stored in an authorized durable private location; a temporary workspace does not suffice. The checkpoint includes messages and must never be committed. `export` writes a restorable JSON checkpoint to stdout; use `umask 077` before redirecting it to a private file. `restore` accepts only this module's JSON table format, not SQL. Preserve its checksum separately if your storage supports integrity checks. Record the verified restore/backup reference in each contact review. If the private store or provider history cannot be read, **stop sends**. After `reserve`, checkpoint the reservation and its attempt ID to durable private storage and verify it before any external send. Then call `confirm-checkpoint --attempt ID --evidence PRIVATE_RECEIPT` to obtain the message envelope. This receipt is an operator attestation; the offline CLI cannot verify a remote store. Also checkpoint after every result or suppression and before changing executors. One coordinator owns the database; workers submit evidence and receive reservations from it. SQLite serializes local writers, not separate copies.

## Daily workflow

1. **Reconcile first.** Import each older daily JSON with `import-legacy` (idempotent by provider message ID). Check the actual provider, not just the local record. Import unanswered/uncertain attempts and suppressions. Any unresolved reservation stays blocked across restarts until its exact provider outcome is resolved. Never infer failure from silence or timeout.
2. **Discover candidates.** Search public creator-authored agent-building tutorials, agent evaluation/reliability articles, AI research newsletters, developer demonstrations and open-source workflow talks. Example themes: agent tool timeout/idempotency; agent repair regressions; evidence review; long-running coding; creative agent collaboration. Prefer a small creator with a specific fit over a famous unrelated account. Do not scrape restricted directories, guess emails or collect private contact data. Follow source terms.
3. **Verify and deduplicate.** Confirm the creator and aliases are the same person/operator using their own cross-links. Identify a publicly offered appropriate contact route and one specific relevant work. Import each with `add`. The same email, profile or identity cannot create a second person. Explicitly add known aliases to prevent cross-channel duplication; unrelated profiles cannot be identified automatically. Resolve identity collisions before outreach. No follower-count threshold or fabricated audience metrics are used.
4. **Review a personalized message.** Address the actual creator; cite their actual work and one ARC question; offer one low-effort criticism or example. Identify the sender and AI assistance honestly. State that this is an unpaid open-source invitation with no expectation of coverage. Include a simple refusal route. No promised coverage, inflated adoption claims or invented compliments. Save `subject`, `body`, `personalization`, `ask`, `opt_out_text` and `sender_identity`; the last two must literally appear in the body. Run `review` only after checking source freshness (within 14 days), provider history, recipient/channel rules and durable private checkpoint (within 24 hours). These are recorded attestations, not automatic legal or factual verification.
5. **Reserve before sending.** `reserve` performs an atomic person-level check. First contacts are blocked by prior confirmed or uncertain attempts on any channel, refusals, replies or bounces. Per-channel rolling-24-hour caps are **zero until a runner configures a verified safe capacity** using `cap`. Pending and uncertain reservations consume capacity until resolved, as do sends and bounces in the last 24 hours; changing the report timezone cannot reset this window. Caps are operational ceilings, not provider guarantees. First persist the returned reservation ID in durable private storage and confirm the checkpoint before sending. Use that ID as the stable operation identity in the sending tool when supported. Recheck live suppression and provider history immediately before the external action; do not keep batches of stale reservations.
6. **Record the actual result.** Use `result` with `sent` and a real provider ID scoped as `provider:account:message-id` plus an evidence link/reference, For a contact form with a successful on-page receipt but no provider message ID, use `accepted` with the actual receipt text/reference; this is reported separately from confirmed message sends. Use `unknown` on ambiguous outcome. When reconciling an uncertain or historical operation, pass the actual provider send timestamp via `--at`, never the time you checked it. `failed` means verified technical failure with no send and requires evidence. A policy rejection is `denied`, not `unknown` or a retriable failure; it blocks automatic retries until authorization is resolved outside this ledger. Preserve prior denials when a separately authorized retry later succeeds. `delivered`, `bounced`, `reply` and `participation` use separate `event` records and evidence. A participation event means a relevant external contribution, not an email reply or generic promotion; verified reuse is a separate `reuse` event. A refusal immediately uses `suppress` and cancels open reservations. Do not mark accepted-as-sent as delivered.
7. **Follow-up conservatively.** The initial default is no follow-ups. An explicitly authorized review may set `followup_allowed`. `reserve --kind followup` additionally requires a confirmed first send, no reply/bounce/refusal, at least seven days since first send and at most one follow-up. A follow-up never counts as a new influencer. Inbound human replies should be read and handled in context, not placed back into the cold sequence.
8. **Report honestly.** `report --day YYYY-MM-DD --timezone Asia/Shanghai --target 150` counts unique first contacts using provider-confirmed timestamps, deduplicated across days/channels, and states the shortfall. The ready queue is an individually eligible candidate pool, not a batch allocation of remaining channel capacity. It separately shows follow-ups, unknown attempts, policy denials, replies, delivery confirmations, relevant contributions, reuse, suppressions and queue size. A day's sent-cohort response rate uses that day's first-send cohort and the observed reply evidence so far; it is not a conversion rate from impressions or proof of incremental growth. No pixel, click tracker or fabricated traffic denominator is added.

To aim above 150 without lowering standards, research a larger qualified pool and diversify permitted channels. The daily target is not a promise of quota, deliverability, candidate supply or recipient consent. If ready candidates or permitted capacity are insufficient, report the exact shortfall and bottleneck. The runner should schedule discovery, reconciliation, permitted execution and an end-of-day report through its existing authorized task system, not a new unapproved service.

## Candidate and review input

`add` reads JSON from `--input` (never a public file):

```json
{"identity":"https://creator.example/about","name":"Example Creator","aliases":["https://video.example/@creator"],"channel":"email","address":"creator@example.org","contact_source":"https://creator.example/contact","relevance_source":"https://creator.example/agent-retries","relevance":"Explains ambiguous retry outcomes.","verified_at":"2026-11-10T08:00:00+00:00"}
```

`review --person PERSON_ID --input ...` accepts:

```json
{"subject":"A concrete retry case","body":"Hi Example, your retry article raises a case in ARC #51. Would you point out one missing failure mode? This is an unpaid invitation, with no expectation of coverage. Reply no and I will not follow up. Sender Name, assisted by AI.","personalization":"The creator-authored retry article explains lost acknowledgments.","ask":"One missing failure mode in ARC #51.","opt_out_text":"Reply no and I will not follow up.","sender_identity":"Sender Name, assisted by AI.","history_checked_at":"2026-11-10T08:00:00+00:00","policy_checked_at":"2026-11-10T08:00:00+00:00","backup_verified_at":"2026-11-10T08:00:00+00:00","history_evidence":"provider-history-query-reference","policy_evidence":"current-channel-rules-and-contact-purpose-reference","backup_evidence":"private-checkpoint-reference","followup_allowed":false}
```

See `--help` for the commands. These examples are fictional and not research candidates.

## Evaluation and correction

Baseline: three initial sends in the existing private ledger; no verified delivery or useful participant outcome. Before/after mechanics: reject duplicate identities/endpoints, serialize reservations, restore private state, preserve ambiguous outcomes and refusals, and reproduce aggregate counts from evidence. Assess benefit with actual attributed relevant replies, first contributions, returns and independently evidenced corrections/reuse; disclose other distribution changes and unknown denominators. Different accounts do not establish independent operators.

Stop an unsafe channel, suppress immediately and correct the evidence record rather than filling a target. Disable sending in the runner to roll back while preserving the private ledger. Revert this module without touching research protocols or public participation. Contact records remain operator-managed private data; there is no automatic deletion policy because dropping suppression history could cause recontact. Any later retention/deletion design must preserve refusal enforcement and applicable data rights.
